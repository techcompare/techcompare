Case File 01

From a Beed Village to the Civil List

Tukaram Haribhau Mundhe was born on 3 June 1975 in Tadsonna, a small village in Maharashtra's drought-prone Beed district, into a farming family. From around the age of eight he split his days between school and fieldwork — fencing, well-digging, sowing, and selling produce alongside his family.

He completed his schooling at a local Zilla Parishad school before moving to Aurangabad for higher studies, where he earned a bachelor's degree spanning History, Political Science and Sociology, and later a master's degree in Political Science from Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar Marathwada University.

After clearing the Maharashtra state civil services exam in 2001, he went on to secure All India Rank 20 in the UPSC Civil Services Examination of 2004 — earning his place in the Indian Administrative Service and, in 2005, a posting to the Maharashtra cadre.

Case File 03

The Record He's Known For

01

The Milk Adulteration Crackdown

As FDA Commissioner from May 2026, he launched one of Maharashtra's largest-ever food safety drives — tightening inspection across dairies, transporters, distributors and retailers statewide.

02

No Heritage Exemptions

His FDA suspended the licence of Mumbai's iconic K. Rustom ice cream parlour after hygiene violations — and publicly held that a brand's legacy doesn't excuse compromising public health.

03

Seizures at Scale

One enforcement operation alone recovered nearly ₹2.93 crore in edible oil from a unit found running on unsafe, rusted machinery with no quality controls.

04

Breaking the Tanker Mafias

Long before the FDA, as a district collector he took on entrenched water-tanker cartels that controlled rural supply — a fight that shaped his reputation early.

05

Digitising the Land Record

He pushed early digitisation of land records and streamlined municipal revenue collection at multiple postings, closing off routine avenues for corruption.

06

The Three-Point Test

Colleagues describe a simple personal checklist behind his decisions: is it legal, is it ethical, and is it in the public interest.

On the record, in fairness: a career built on this pace hasn't been without cost. Mundhe has faced no-confidence motions from elected bodies in Navi Mumbai and Nashik, an FIR tied to a lockdown-era dispute, and criticism from some quarters for being uncompromising to a fault. Supporters read the same friction as the price of genuine reform.
